# Security Payroll Demo — client UAT walkthrough

Prepared 2026-08-08 from `UAT-2026-07-03.md`, `UPDATES.md`, `tracker.html`, and the completed browser UAT runs.

## Executive summary

The original July UAT notes produced thirteen requirements. Twelve are implemented and verified in the demo tenant. Patrol logging is the only feature not built because it needs a client decision on the kind of proof they expect from a patrol system.

The system now has a clear operational chain:

`roster → attendance → approval → payroll → payslip → client statement`

It also separates operational authority from financial authority: supervisors can report what happened; payroll and management approve the actions that affect money or employment.

## What to say at the start

"We did not add screens just to tick boxes. Each change was built around the actual control it needs: attendance is confirmed per shift, disciplinary deductions need three people, leave is charged only against planned work, and financial documents are calculated from ledger data rather than generated by AI."

## The key design choices to explain

### 1. Sunday work is contract-based, not a per-person checkbox

The company’s rule is that every guard has already agreed to a 1.5× rate for rostered Sundays. That is why the rate is consistent across guards. A replacement is different: they were not rostered to work that Sunday, so their cover shift is 2× and visibly separated on the payslip.

This was tested live:

- EMP035: `Sunday (1.5x)` for 7 hours.
- EMP111: `Sunday (1.5x)` for 12 hours.
- EMP108: `Sunday call-in (2x)` for 12 hours, $456, after covering an absent guard.

### 2. Payroll only uses approved attendance and confirmed disciplinary actions

This keeps a supervisor’s initial report from silently changing a person’s pay. Attendance is confirmed at shift level, and a fine stays out of payroll until three different people have completed the record, verification and confirmation chain.

### 3. Leave follows the roster, not a weekday assumption

Security staff do not all work Monday to Saturday. The correct rule is: if a guard was scheduled, an approved leave day is charged; if they were off, it is not. This prevents both Sunday overcharging and weekday assumptions from producing the wrong balance.

### 4. Roster welfare is visible rather than quietly impossible

Ten rest days, paired days off and the 240-hour ceiling affect each other. The system reports the conflict honestly and explains the exception rather than producing a roster that looks compliant but is operationally impossible.

### 5. Financial outputs are deterministic

Payslips and statements are generated from the payroll and ledger calculations. The AI assistant is not trusted to generate a financial document where a wrong number would be disputable.

## Recommended client demo order

Aim for 35–45 minutes. Keep this order; it tells one coherent operational story.

1. **Dashboard and roles — 3 minutes**
   - Explain Supervisor, Payroll and CEO/management responsibilities.
   - Point out that Payroll runs payroll; the CEO account can review and administer settings.

2. **Schedule and welfare controls — 6 minutes**
   - Open Schedule and the Roster rules panel.
   - Show off-day pairing, rest-day findings and monthly-hour findings.
   - Open Admin Settings and show the 240-hour enforcement toggle.

3. **Daily Muster — 7 minutes**
   - Show Day and Night cards.
   - Mark an absence and record its reason.
   - Show Find replacement and explain the weekly-hour screening.
   - Point out the repeat-AWOL prompt and supervisor offence flag.

4. **People, discipline and exits — 7 minutes**
   - Open an employee profile: disciplinary history, contract Sunday wording, and Employment exit.
   - Show the three-person chain and explain that unconfirmed fines do not affect pay.

5. **Leave — 7 minutes**
   - Submit or open a leave request.
   - Show roster impact, independent approval, balance ledger and cover assignment.
   - Explain cancellation reversal and the separate leave lines on payroll/payslips.

6. **Payroll and payslips — 8 minutes**
   - Run payroll as the payroll role.
   - Show the attendance/disciplinary warning indicators.
   - Open the three Sunday payslips above.
   - Show transport proration on a payslip if a suitable absence record is available.

7. **Client statements — 4 minutes**
   - Open a client statement, show running balance and aging, record a partial payment, export PDF.

8. **Patrol decision — 3 minutes**
   - End with the only unbuilt item and get a concrete decision.

## What still needs a decision or final follow-up

### Patrol logging — client decision needed

Recommendation: build site checkpoints and timestamped patrol-round visits, designed for a phone to scan a **QR code** fixed at each checkpoint. QR is the best first version: cheap, works on ordinary phones, fast to deploy and does not require special NFC wristbands or readers. NFC can be added later for higher-assurance sites, but it raises hardware and compatibility requirements.

Decide with the client:

- Is phone-based QR scanning acceptable as evidence of a patrol?
- Which sites and checkpoints are in the first rollout?
- Do they need missed-checkpoint alerts, client reports, photos, GPS proximity, or offline use?

### Monthly cap policy

The 240-hour enforcement toggle is live. Before relying on it operationally, run one controlled test that tries to confirm or schedule a guard beyond 240 hours and records the actual block message. Also decide whether the requested **20 monthly overtime hours** is a contractual rule that needs a separate payroll-run control; it is not currently enforced as a monthly limit.

### Public-holiday proof

The payroll logic and payslip label retain the 2× public-holiday rate. For a presentation-quality evidence pack, keep one payslip with actual approved public-holiday hours at 2× alongside the Sunday examples.

## Before the client session

- Use a clean browser session and confirm the demo server is running.
- Keep the Payroll, Attendance, Schedule, Leave, Employee, Clients and Admin Settings pages ready.
- Use only the dedicated UAT period and clearly label it as test data.
- Have the three Sunday payslips ready: EMP035, EMP111 and EMP108.
- Do not finalise/lock a UAT payroll period during the demo.
- Be direct about patrol logging and the monthly overtime rule: these are the remaining product/policy decisions, not hidden gaps.
